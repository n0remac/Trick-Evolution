# A2 bounded Monte Carlo opponents

A2 is implemented on `feature/a2-monte-carlo-rollouts`. The normal server constructor selects A2 for bot seats; `NewServerWithBotOptions` can select the A1 heuristic for fixtures or supply an A2 budget and seed factory. A1 remains the fallback and the policy used by simulated players. The local `main` branch was fast-forwarded to A1 commit `44c8529` before this branch was created. GitHub `origin/main` and the public D2 deployment were not changed.

## Information boundary

The room records accepted public card plays and trick winners for the current hand. Each play stores its lead suit and whether Follow Suit was active *at that play*. This history resets when the next hand begins. Under the room lock, a bot receives a copied `PlayerView` (public state and its own cards) plus that public history. A2's decision function accepts only that observation, the catalog, an injected seed, budget, and cancellation context. It never accepts the live `MatchState`, opponent hands, undealt deck, future rule order, or shuffle records. A fixed-seed test changes the true opponent hand and undealt deck while preserving the observation and verifies identical candidate scores and choice.

The sampler removes the bot's hand and all publicly played cards from the stable 52-card set. It assigns unknown cards to opponent hand slots according to public hand counts; the rest form a synthetic undealt deck. It infers a void suit only from an off-suit play made while Follow Suit was active. No Follow Required does not imply a void. A seeded shuffle and bounded augmenting-path matching satisfy those constraints. Invalid counts, duplicate cards, inconsistent history, contradictory voids, and impossible assignments cause a legal A1 fallback.

Each sample builds a synthetic `domain.MatchState` from public scores, seats, dealer, turn, active rule field, current trick, captured public plays, and sampled cards. It independently shuffles unrevealed supported rules for the synthetic future rule deck. The simulation stops at the end of the current hand; a reveal on hands 3/6/9 can occur on the last simulated `domain.Step`, after that hand's scoring.

## Candidate evaluation and limits

`domain.LegalCards` supplies candidates. Every candidate in a comparison round uses the same sampled world. A2 applies the candidate and finishes the hand using pure `domain.Step` and the A1 policy on each simulated seat's own synthetic view. It compares either bot cumulative score minus the strongest opponent score (default), or bot points earned in the hand. Ties favor A1's card, then stable card ID. Only complete comparison rounds contribute scores.

The default is 8 samples per card, 1,000 total `Step` calls, 3,000 sampling nodes per world, and 80 ms per decision. The injected `RolloutBudget` supports 1–64 samples, 1–5,000 steps, 1–20,000 sample nodes, and 1 ms–1 second. Production seeds come from a cryptographic source; the private decision log records room, revision, chosen card, seed, objective, complete sample count, step count, stop reason, and candidate score sums. It contains no hidden hands or synthetic deals. Fixed seeds and budgets reproduce decisions when the same observation is available. Match state and observations are not persisted across process restarts, so production log lines alone are not a durable replay archive.

A room-owned context cancels search when the room closes. The worker computes outside room locks, checks cancellation during sampling and every rollout step, and rechecks revision and turn under locks before committing. Wall-time expiration, cancellation, invalid observation, failed sample, or zero complete rounds use the legal A1 choice. A forced card skips search.

## Verification and limits

- `GOCACHE=/tmp/trick-a2-go-cache /usr/local/go/bin/go test ./domain ./gameui -count=1` passed on the Pi. The `gameui` suite includes fixed-seed 2- and 4-seat rollout decisions that complete hand-boundary simulations, card conservation, true-hidden-state independence, Follow/No Follow void tests, impossible sampling, budget and cancellation fallback, and safe option validation.
- Black-box HTTP/WebSocket fixtures play all ten hands with one and three A2 bots, check every transition against `domain.Step`, final scores `[17, 33]` and `[10, 16, 16, 8]`, reveals at hands 3/6/9, reconnect, winners, privacy, and post-game rejection. The A1 two- and four-seat fixtures remain pinned to heuristic mode and their known scores.
- Simulated unknown deals are constraint-consistent but not sampled uniformly from all valid deals. The policy optimizes only the current hand against A1 simulated play; it does not search future hands or model human strategy. The 80 ms production cap can choose A1 fallback on a slow host. There is no difficulty selector in the UI or persisted match replay.
- A broader `go test ./...` attempt ran the A2 packages but could not build the unrelated `db` package because its missing dependencies needed writes to the read-only default Go module cache. The focused `domain` and `gameui` suites are the A2 verification gate; the broader suite remains unverified in this sandbox. A race-enabled run was not performed.
