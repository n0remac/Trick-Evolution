# Pitch and Whist expansion: phase 2

Phase 2 adds a competitive auction and contracts as optional evolutions. The finite default still starts with five cards, one point per trick, individual scores, no trump, and its original 18-card draw. The catalog is version 9. New auction cards are available as ordered starting selections in finite Game Rules and in the curated Endless deck. Selecting the auction in a finite room also puts the two bid-metric cards and Set the Bidder into that match's draw; prerequisites still decide when each can appear.

## Supported four-point Pitch variant

Select four seats, six cards per player, Pitch the Trump, No Points for Tricks, High Trump Scores, Low Trump Scores, Trump Jack Scores, Game Point, and Competitive Auction. Set the Bidder is optional. This is the supported **six-card, four-point Pitch scoring variant**: High belongs to the original holder of the highest dealt trump; Low belongs to the captor of the lowest dealt trump unless Keep Low is active; Jack belongs to its captor if the trump Jack was dealt; Game belongs to the unique highest captured-card tally (10=10, A=4, K=3, Q=2, J=1). A tied Game tally earns no point. Undealt cards are out of play. The first card led by the auction winner sets trump, including on trick one. There is no draw, discard, or forced trump lead. Partner Across can independently create opposite-seat teams. This exact setup has legal point bids 2 through 4.

## Auction and contract rules

Competitive Auction starts **after the deal and before the first play**. The player left of the dealer acts first. On a turn, bid a whole number strictly above the current high bid, within the displayed bounds, or pass permanently. A displaced bidder can raise again on a later turn. The auction ends when every other seat has passed or the maximum bid is reached. If everyone passes, the dealer is assigned the minimum bid and leads, even if the dealer passed. Otherwise the highest bidder leads. A new reveal, removal, or replacement takes effect at the next deal, never during the auction or hand. Early game end during an auction leaves the hand unsettled and applies no contract penalty.

The hidden default metric is **Bid for Points**, so Competitive Auction alone always has a metric. Bid for Tricks replaces it; Bid for Points can later restore it. These metric cards require the auction and do not require partnerships. A bid for tricks measures the bidder's captured tricks, or both partners' actual captured tricks. A bid for points measures the bidder's net scoring awards for the completed hand, or the sum of both partners' awards. A team-wide award appears once in that sum. Negative heart penalties reduce the point total; tied or undealt hand awards add zero. Ordinary trick points, Whist majority awards, and Last Trick Bonus count when active. A successful contract leaves normal earned scores intact.

The maximum trick bid is the number of tricks in the effective deal. The maximum point bid is the sum of the **upper bounds** of active positive awards: one per trick, or the maximum Whist majority award, plus one each for High, Low, Jack, and Game, plus two for Last Trick Bonus. Negative awards do not raise the cap. Some legal high bids may be unattainable for a particular deal because the relevant cards were not dealt, and that risk belongs to the bidder. The minimum is one except when Pitch the Trump, No Points for Tricks, and all four Pitch awards are active with no other positive awards; then it is two. An auction with no possible positive hand points is rejected at setup or rule selection. Keep One reduces the trick count used for both metrics.

Set the Bidder applies only at completed-hand settlement. If the contract misses, the bidder's (or bidding team's) earned awards for that hand are **replaced** by one penalty of `-bid` match points. This includes replacing negative awards on the bidder side; it is not an additional subtraction from their earned total. Opponents retain all awards they earned. The scorecard shows the bid, actual result, success or failure, and penalty. Without Set the Bidder, a miss is recorded but normal awards remain. There is no target-score victory or predictive Spades-style per-player bid.

## Team and Endless accounting

Partner Across and Competitive Auction are independent stage-six strategies. Both enter the round 6+ curated options when established positive scoring is active; neither requires the other or a particular predecessor card. Table choices prioritize a missing partnership and then a missing auction, so both remain reachable. Drafts offer both when eligible. Bid metric and Set the Bidder follow the auction. The guaranteed round-two Expanded Hand can be replaced in a later draft by Six-Card Hand or Whist Full Deal. Selecting either retires active Expanded Hand and Keep One Card at the boundary; selecting the other hand replacement later retires the current one. Round-seven and later draft slots alternate those hand replacements while available. No hand changes midhand.

TeamScores remains the explicit ledger for the current partnership interval. It receives each award once and starts at zero when a partnership starts. Personal and Endless run scores retain their historical seat attribution. Team-wide Game, Whist majority, and Set the Bidder awards are attributed to the partner with the smaller running balance of **team-wide attributed points**; hand-number parity breaks a tie. This is deterministic and avoids Phase 1's permanent first-seat bias. Seat-specific trick, High, Low, Jack, and heart awards stay with the relevant player. A team-wide award is still a single award: the team ledger and the one attributed personal ledger each change once, and the team scorecard sums personal awards once. Historical Phase 1 scores are not redistributed. The balance can favor a partner after a large penalty, which keeps signed attribution closer over time; it does not imply equal individual skill or retroactive sharing.

Rule removal is host-authorized between hands. A rule with an active dependent must have that dependent removed or replaced first. The field keeps removed entries for review; the next deal uses the baseline or remaining controller. Removing the auction stops future auctions, and a completed contract stays in its historical scorecard.

## Replay and verification

Auction calls, winning bid, contract, metric, and result live in the match snapshot and public view; only each player's dealt cards remain private. A reconnect receives the current turn and legal bounds. Accepted bid/pass actions increment the same revision as card plays; stale HTMX revisions, wrong turns, illegal bids, and repeated command IDs are rejected. Bot bids use only the public auction and the bot's own projected hand. Automatic deals open the auction when the next hand's rules call for it. Endless bot-seat takeover also transfers the public auction seat identity. Matchmaking remains restricted to between-hand boundaries.

Version-eight Phase 1 snapshots require `MigratePhase1Snapshot` before use with catalog 9. It appends the hidden default bid metric, preserves existing scores, scorecards, cards, and rule history, and adds the four new Endless draw cards in stable order. Old snapshots are otherwise rejected by the version check. A version-nine shuffle record replays the same first deal and auction. Focused domain and HTTP/WebSocket tests cover both metrics, individual and team contracts, success, failure, all-pass fallback, bounds, illegal actions, bot continuation, stage reachability, hand replacement, score attribution, migration, and replay.

Phase 3 remains target-score victory, named Pitch/Whist game recognition, and predictive Spades bidding. Advanced Pitch variants such as draw/discard, Jokers, and off-suit trumps also remain outside this phase.
